# Categories of Design Patterns

## Why Categories Exist
- Every object-oriented system must solve three fundamental problems:
  1. How are objects created?
  2. How are objects organised?
  3. How do objects collaborate?

## Creational Patterns
- Focus on object creation.
- Hide or simplify complex construction.
- Decide who creates objects and which implementation is used.
- Examples: Singleton, Factory Method, Abstract Factory, Builder, Prototype.

## Structural Patterns
- Focus on organising existing objects.
- Build larger structures from smaller components.
- Emphasise composition and relationships.
- Examples: Adapter, Bridge, Composite, Decorator, Facade, Flyweight, Proxy.

## Behavioural Patterns
- Focus on communication and collaboration between objects.
- Define how responsibilities and decisions flow.
- Examples: Strategy, Observer, State, Command, Mediator, Chain of Responsibility.

## Relationship to SOLID
- Creational patterns often leverage DIP, OCP and encapsulation.
- Structural patterns rely heavily on composition, low coupling and high cohesion.
- Behavioural patterns rely on interfaces, polymorphism and substitutability.

## Mental Model
- Every object has a lifecycle:
  Create → Organise → Collaborate.
- Choose a pattern family based on which stage contains the design problem.
