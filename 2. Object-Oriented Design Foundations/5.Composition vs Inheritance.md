# Composition vs Inheritance

## Inheritance
- Models an "is-a" relationship.
- Represents specialisation.
- The child inherits behaviour from the parent.
- Appropriate only when the child is genuinely a specialised form of the parent.

## Composition
- Models a "has-a" or "uses-a" relationship.
- Represents collaboration between objects.
- Objects delegate work to other objects.
- Encourages modularity and flexibility.

## Key Differences
- Inheritance creates a type hierarchy.
- Composition assembles behaviour from collaborating components.

## Why Composition Is Often Preferred
- Components can be replaced independently.
- Changes are localised.
- Responsibilities remain focused.
- Systems are easier to extend and maintain.

## Decision Questions
1. Is X truly a specialised form of Y? → Consider inheritance.
2. Does X simply use or contain Y? → Prefer composition.

Mental Model
- Inheritance is about family relationships.
- Composition is about teamwork.
