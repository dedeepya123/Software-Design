# Interface Segregation Principle (ISP)

## Definition
- Clients should not be forced to depend on interfaces they do not use.

## Core Idea
- An interface should represent one cohesive capability or role.
- Avoid creating "God interfaces" that bundle unrelated operations.

## Why ISP Exists
- Large interfaces force implementations to provide behaviour they may not support.
- This often leads to placeholder implementations, exceptions, or confusing designs.

## Relationship to Other Principles
- SRP: One responsibility per class.
- ISP: One cohesive responsibility per interface.
- LSP: Focused interfaces make it easier for implementations to honour their contracts.
- OCP: Well-designed interfaces provide clean extension points.

## Warning Signs
- Implementations throwing `NotImplementedError` for required methods.
- Interfaces with unrelated operations.
- Difficulty describing the interface's purpose in one sentence.

## Mental Model
- An interface is a role or capability, not a container for every related method.
