# Liskov Substitution Principle (LSP)

## Definition
- Objects of a subtype should be usable wherever objects of the parent type are expected without changing the correctness of the program.

## Core Idea
- LSP is about behavioural compatibility, not inheritance syntax.
- A subtype must honour the contract established by its parent or interface.

## Behavioural Contract
- Preserve the expected meaning of methods.
- Do not introduce surprising behaviour.
- Do not require stricter inputs than promised.
- Do not violate expected outcomes.

## Relationship to Previous Lessons
- Interfaces define contracts.
- Polymorphism relies on interchangeable implementations.
- OCP depends on safe substitution.
- LSP ensures that substitutions remain correct.

## Warning Signs
- Methods that throw "not supported" exceptions despite being required by the interface.
- Subclasses that change the meaning of inherited methods.
- Inheritance hierarchies where the subtype cannot fulfil the parent's promises.

## Mental Model
- A subtype is making a promise:
  "You can use me anywhere you use my parent, and your program will still behave correctly."
