# Abstraction

## Definition
- Abstraction is the process of focusing on the essential behaviour of a system while ignoring unnecessary implementation details.

## Why Abstraction Exists
- Humans cannot reason about every low-level detail simultaneously.
- Abstraction reduces cognitive load by allowing us to think at the right level.

## Key Insight
- Abstraction does not remove details.
- It hides details that are irrelevant to the current task while exposing meaningful behaviour.

## Examples
- Functions hide implementation behind a name.
- Classes represent real-world or domain concepts.
- APIs expose behaviour without revealing internal implementation.
- Frameworks like PyTorch abstract complex tensor operations behind simple interfaces.

## Relationship with Other Concepts
- Modularity divides systems into manageable units.
- Cohesion ensures each unit has a focused purpose.
- Coupling controls dependencies between units.
- Abstraction defines what a unit exposes to the outside world.

## Mental Model
- Abstraction is like zooming in and out on a map: every level is valid, but each shows only the details relevant at that level.
